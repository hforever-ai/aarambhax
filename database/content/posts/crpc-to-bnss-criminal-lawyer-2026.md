The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 on **1 July 2024**. For practising criminal lawyers in Chhattisgarh, this is not a cosmetic rebrand. Section numbers have changed, several procedures have been restructured, and a number of substantive timelines are now mandatory rather than directory.

This post is a working reference: the section-by-section CrPC ↔ BNSS mapping every criminal advocate at the CG High Court should keep handy. Use it before drafting bail applications, quashing petitions, revisions, and appeals.

## When to use which code

The cardinal rule:

- **FIR registered on or after 1 July 2024** → BNSS provisions apply throughout investigation, trial, and appeal.
- **FIR registered before 1 July 2024** → CrPC continues to apply for that case (saving clauses preserve pre-existing investigations).

If you are uncertain, the date stamp on the FIR is the safest reference point. Citing the wrong code at the filing counter is a common reason drafts get returned in the CG HC e-filing portal.

## Appeals and revisions — quick lookup

For appellate work at CG HC Bilaspur, these are the case-type prefixes you'll encounter most often:

- **M.Cr.C.** — Misc. Criminal Case (covers regular bail under BNSS §483, anticipatory bail under BNSS §482, quashing under BNSS §528, transfer petitions under BNSS §448)
- **Cr.A.** — Criminal Appeal (BNSS §415 / CrPC §374)
- **Cr.R.** — Criminal Revision (BNSS §438 / CrPC §397)
- **Acq.A.** — Acquittal Appeal (BNSS §415 / CrPC §378)

## Five high-impact procedural changes worth memorising

Beyond the section renumbering, BNSS introduces several substantive shifts that affect day-to-day district practice:

1. **Mandatory forensic investigation for offences punishable with 7+ years** (BNSS §176(3)). Failure to invoke a forensic team is now a ground for challenging the investigation itself.
2. **Time-bound trial** — adjournments capped at two per side (BNSS §346 proviso), and judgment to be delivered within 45 days of conclusion of hearing (BNSS §392(1)).
3. **Electronic communication and trial-by-video-conferencing** are explicitly recognised throughout BNSS (§§ 173(4), 180(3), 254(2), 351, 530). Useful for challenging witness summons issued only by physical post.
4. **Plea bargaining expanded** under BNSS §289–§300 to cover offences punishable with up to 7 years.
5. **Default bail clarified** — BNSS §187(3) expressly states the accused *shall be released on bail* if charge-sheet not filed within 60/90 days, removing earlier interpretive doubt.

## Common mistakes when transitioning

- **Citing IPC sections in a BNSS-era FIR draft.** If the FIR was registered after 1 July 2024, the substantive offences should be from BNS (e.g., §319 for cheating, not IPC §420).
- **Mixing CrPC §439 and BNSS §483 in a single bail application.** Pick the operative code based on the FIR date and stay consistent throughout the draft.
- **Quoting case-law citations under old section numbers without explanation.** Pre-1-July-2024 SC judgments cite CrPC §482 (not BNSS §528). Cite the precedent as decided, then add a parenthetical note: "*now reflected in §528 of the BNSS, 2023*."
- **Using "Pratham Suchna Report" as a translated term where you actually need it in English.** The CG HC accepts English translations with original Devanagari in brackets — `First Information Report (Pratham Suchna Report / प्रथम सूचना रिपोर्ट)`. This is the convention the Verifier expects.

## Practical drafting tips for CG HC e-filing

1. The CG HC e-filing portal accepts case-type prefixes from a fixed list. Use **M.Cr.C.** for bail and quashing matters, not "M.Cr.A." or other variants.
2. The first line of the petition must read `IN THE HIGH COURT OF CHHATTISGARH AT BILASPUR`. Watch for autocorrect changing it to "Chattisgarh" (one t).
3. State of Chhattisgarh as Respondent No. 1 must include the full address: *through the Secretary, Department of Home, Mahanadi Bhawan, Nava Raipur, Atal Nagar, Dist. Raipur (C.G.).* Aarambhax auto-fills this.
4. The Synopsis and List of Dates must come before the cause-title — not after. This is a CG HC Rules 2007 requirement that filing-counter staff specifically check.
